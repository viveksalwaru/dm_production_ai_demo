Pick a problem

Cleaning water for the town

River intake and pumps

Draw raw water from the river. A sensor checks how muddy it is (turbidity).

Mixing and clarifier

A chemical called alum makes dirt clump together. In the big round tank the clumps sink to the bottom.

Sand filters

Catch the fine dirt left over. They are washed backwards (backwash) when they clog.

Chlorine and storage

A small dose of chlorine kills germs. Clean water is stored and pumped up to the overhead tank.

Pipes and meters

Carry water to every home. Flow and pressure meters in each ward show where water goes and where it leaks.

Treating waste water

Sewers and screens

Carry used water from homes to the sewage plant. Screens remove plastic, rags and grit.

Aeration tank and blowers

Blowers bubble air through the water so good bacteria can eat the dirt. They use about half the plant’s electricity.

Settling tank and sludge

The bacteria settle out as sludge. Sludge goes to a digester that makes biogas.

Treated water reuse

Clean enough water goes to parks, farms and industry instead of wasting fresh water. The rest returns safely to the river.

Control room and cloud AI

The SCADA system collects every reading on site. The cloud AI learns how the whole cycle behaves and suggests or makes the best settings.

Six ways the AI helps

Right chemical dose

Alum and chlorine follow the river’s quality minute by minute, so water stays safe without wasting chemicals.

Cheaper aeration

Blowers give the bacteria exactly the air they need, often saving a quarter of the plant’s power.

Find leaks from night flow

At night almost nobody uses water, so extra flow means a leak. The AI points to the ward and lane.

Pump when power is cheap

It predicts tomorrow’s demand and fills tanks during off-peak hours.

Warn before limits are crossed

Outlet quality trends are watched so staff can act before a pollution limit is broken.

Fix pumps before they fail

Vibration, heat and current show early wear, so repairs are planned, not emergencies.

All numbers in this demo are examples for a town of about 30,000 people, with power at ₹9 per kWh.
