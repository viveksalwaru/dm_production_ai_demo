The main parts, in plain words

Ships and ports

Bring parts and products from overseas. Port data shows queues and delays.

Suppliers

Make the parts the factory needs. Their portal shares stock and delivery dates.

Factory

Turns parts into products. Its systems report output and stock every hour.

Distribution centre (DC)

The big warehouse that feeds the stores. It holds buffer stock and a cold room.

Trucks with trackers

GPS shows where every truck is. Refrigerated trucks also report the cargo temperature.

Stores and tills (POS)

Every bill shows what is selling, store by store, minute by minute.

Control tower

The planning team’s system that collects all this data and sends it to the cloud.

Cloud AI

Forecasts demand, spots disruptions early and finds the cheapest way to keep shelves full.

Six ways the AI helps

Supply chains are long, and a problem far away shows up weeks later as an empty shelf. The AI connects the dots early.

Forecast demand

Uses sales, weather, festivals and promotions to predict what each store will sell.

See delays early

Ship trackers and port data warn of late arrivals days before they hit the factory.

Move stock to where it sells

Shifts products from slow stores to busy ones before anyone runs out.

Plan routes and loads

Builds daily truck routes that cost less and arrive on time.

Protect the cold chain

Watches temperatures in every refrigerated truck and acts in minutes.

Line up backup suppliers

Knows which other supplier can deliver if one fails, and how fast.

All numbers in this demo are examples for a consumer goods company with one factory and 120 stores.
