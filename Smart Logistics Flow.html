The main parts, in plain words

Shelves (racks)

Tall metal shelves full of products. A computer system (called a WMS) remembers exactly which shelf holds each item.

Little robots (AMRs)

Small orange robots that drive around carrying boxes from the shelves to the packing tables, so people walk less.

Packing tables

Workers put each order into a box and stick on the address label.

Moving belt (conveyor)

Carries packed boxes across the building to the right truck, like an airport baggage belt.

Cameras and scanners

Watch the belt for jams, read the barcode on every box and check what goes into each truck.

Loading doors and trucks

Trucks back up to doors on the side of the building. Boxes are loaded and driven to customers.

Control tower

The warehouse’s main computer. It runs the robots and belts and sends all data to the cloud.

Cloud AI

The “planner”. It watches everything at once and keeps changing the plan so orders go out faster and cheaper.

The three situations, explained simply

No logistics knowledge needed. Each card says what is going on, what usually happens without AI, and what the AI does differently.

1. Sudden rush of orders

What’s going on: A sale starts and orders triple. Almost everyone buys the same few items, but those items are on the far shelves.

Without AI: Workers keep walking long distances. The manager notices too late and calls in extra staff, which costs money and takes hours.

With AI: It spots the popular items within minutes, moves them next to the packing tables, and puts every robot to work on them.

The number: orders sent per hour go from 820 to 1,150, so the day’s orders still leave by 4 PM.

2. Belt blocked

What’s going on: One oversized box gets stuck on the moving belt. Every box behind it stops.

Without AI: Someone has to notice the pile, walk over, find the cause and fix it. Meanwhile hundreds of boxes miss their truck.

With AI: The camera sees the pile in seconds. The AI sends the boxes along a spare belt and sends the technician a photo of the stuck box.

The number: boxes running late drop from 300 an hour to 20.

3. Trucks leaving half-empty

What’s going on: Boxes are put into trucks in whatever order they arrive, leaving gaps, like a badly packed suitcase.

Without AI: Trucks leave only about 60 % full, so more trucks (and more money) are needed every night.

With AI: It works out where each box should go to fill the space, and shows the loaders on their handheld screens.

The number: the truck goes from 62 % to 91 % full, so one truck fewer is needed tonight, saving about ₹18,000.

So what exactly is the AI doing?

In all three situations the AI does the same four things a very good manager would do, but in seconds, all day, without getting tired.

1. Notices

It watches orders, shelves, robots, cameras and trucks at the same time, and sees a problem the moment it starts.

2. Understands why

It finds the cause: popular items on far shelves, one stuck box, gaps inside the truck.

3. Compares options

It tries several fixes in its “head” and picks the one that saves the most time and money, rejecting slow or costly ones.

4. Acts and reports

It sends new instructions to robots, belts and workers, then tells the manager what it did. People can always say no.

Other everyday jobs the AI helps with

Beyond the three situations above, the same AI quietly improves the warehouse all day.

Keep popular items close

Items that sell a lot are always kept near the packing tables.

Group similar orders

One trip to the shelves collects items for many orders at once.

Send robots where they are needed

Robots go wherever the work is, not on a fixed route.

Spot problems in seconds

Cameras notice jams and broken equipment before trucks are late.

Fill every truck

Packing plans fit more boxes, so fewer trucks are needed.

Guess tomorrow’s workload

Predicts how many orders will come, so the right number of people are called in.
