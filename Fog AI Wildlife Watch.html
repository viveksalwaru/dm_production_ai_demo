The main parts, in plain words

Field cameras

Four infrared cameras send live video over cable to one place.

Network switch

Combines the four camera feeds and sends them down one fast cable.

Fog workstation

A strong computer with a graphics card (GPU), housed in a small hut near the forest. It runs the AI on all feeds at once.

Siren and lights

Triggered directly by the workstation, with no internet needed.

Cloud gateway

Receives alerts, keeps the record and checks that the workstation is alive.

Ranger app

Shows the alert, the photo and a map pin on the rangers’ phones.

Edge or fog?

Both keep the AI close to the forest, so the siren does not wait for the internet. They differ in where the AI computer sits.

What the fog AI does

Keeping one strong computer close to the cameras gives fast decisions and easy upkeep, as long as that computer is looked after.

Watches all cameras at once

One GPU checks every frame from every camera, day and night.

Puts urgent feeds first

When every camera is busy, the feed most likely to show a leopard is checked first.

Easy to upgrade

A better AI model only needs installing on one machine.

Keeps video local

Video stays near the forest. Only small alerts go to the cloud.

Ignores false alarms

Deer, cattle and people do not set off the siren.

Cloud watches the watcher

If the workstation stops, the cloud notices and keeps the cameras covered.
