The main parts, in plain words

Panels and strings

Panels turn sunlight into DC electricity. About 20 panels are wired in a row called a string. This plant has 48 strings.

String combiner boxes

Join several strings together, with a fuse for each. They also measure the current of every string.

Inverters

Turn DC power from the panels into AC power, like the supply at home. Each block of panels has one.

Transformer and grid line

Step the voltage up so the power can travel on the grid. The export meter counts every unit sold.

Weather station

Measures sunlight (irradiance), panel and air temperature, wind and rain. This tells the AI how much power the plant should be making.

Drone with thermal camera

Flies over the plant and sees heat. Faulty panels show up as hot spots.

Cleaning robots

Move along the rows with soft brushes and very little water, usually at night.

Control room (SCADA)

Collects all readings on site, controls the inverters and sends the data to the cloud.

Cloud AI

Knows how much power each block should make right now, spots the gap, finds the cause and decides the most profitable fix.

Six ways the AI raises output

A solar plant rarely breaks all at once. It loses a few percent here and there. The AI finds those small losses and fixes them fast.

Expected vs actual, every minute

Weather data tells the AI what each block should make. Any gap is spotted the same day.

Clean only when it pays

Cleaning costs money. The AI cleans each block when the lost power is worth more than the cleaning.

Find a dead string in minutes

One string out of 48 is easy to miss by eye. The AI points to the exact string and fuse.

Read drone heat maps

Thousands of thermal images are checked automatically. Each hot panel gets a row and table number.

Predict inverter trouble

Rising temperature, fan noise or insulation alarms are caught before the inverter trips.

Forecast tomorrow

Accurate generation forecasts help schedule power with the grid and avoid penalties.
