The main parts, in plain words

Solar camera poles

Four cameras with infrared light watch the corridor day and night, powered by a solar panel and battery.

Edge AI node

A small, rugged computer on each pole. It runs the animal-recognition model on every frame, right there.

Mesh radio links

Nearby nodes can pass video to each other, so a working node can cover for a broken one.

Siren and lights

Scare the animal away from the village. Triggered straight from the node, with no internet needed.

Backup radio

A long-range radio link to the ranger post for when the internet is down.

Cloud gateway

Receives small alerts, keeps the record, watches node health and sends updates.

Ranger app

Shows the alert, the photo and a map pin on the rangers’ phones.

Edge or fog?

Both keep the AI close to the forest, so the siren does not wait for the internet. They differ in where the AI computer sits.

What the edge AI does

Putting the AI on the pole means decisions happen where the animal is, not in a far-away data centre.

Recognises animals in a blink

Each frame is checked on the pole in milliseconds, day or night.

Works without internet

The siren and lights do not depend on a network connection.

Sends tiny alerts, not video

A 40 KB snapshot and location instead of megabytes of video saves data and battery.

Covers for a failed neighbour

If one node crashes, the mesh routes its camera to the next node.

Ignores false alarms

Cattle, dogs, people and swaying branches do not set off the siren.

Gets smarter over time

Improved models are sent to all nodes overnight from the cloud.
