The main parts, in plain words

Chillers

The heart of the system. Big machines in the plant room that make cold water, usually around 6–8 °C. They use most of the HVAC power.

Cooling towers

On the roof. They throw the building’s heat out into the air, using fans and evaporating water.

Pumps and pipes

Pumps push cold water up to the air handlers and warm water to the cooling towers.

Air handlers (AHUs)

Big fan boxes. Air passes over cold-water coils, gets cooled and filtered, and is blown into the floors through ducts.

Ducts and air dampers

Ducts carry cool air to each floor. Dampers (VAV boxes) open or close to send more or less air where it is needed.

Sensors and meters
  • Room temperature and humidity
  • CO₂ (fresh air)
  • People counters
  • Water temperature and flow
  • Energy meters on chillers, pumps, fans
  • Outside weather
Building controller (BMS)

The local brain on site. It runs the equipment safely, collects all readings and passes them to the cloud.

Cloud AI

Learns how the building behaves, checks weather and tariffs, and keeps choosing the settings that use the least energy while keeping people comfortable.

Six ways the AI saves energy

Each one is small. Running all of them every minute, every day, is where the big savings come from.

Cool where people are

Empty floors and meeting rooms get less air. Busy ones get more.

Follow the weather

On mild days, make the water a little less cold. Chillers work much less.

Run the right chillers

One well-loaded chiller often beats two half-loaded ones.

Slow fans and pumps

Running a fan at 80 % speed uses only about half the power of full speed.

Beat the peak tariff

Pre-cool the building before expensive hours, then coast through them.

Catch faults early

Dirty filters, stuck valves and leaking dampers waste energy quietly. The AI flags them.
